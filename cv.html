Research

Who Chooses Your Hospital? Primary-Care Referrals and Hospital Merger Review

Job Market Paper

Hospital merger review relies on demand models in which patients choose among all nearby hospitals. In HMOs and other gatekeeper plans, however, patients reach a hospital through referrals that begin at their primary-care practice, and each practice sends its patients to a small set of hospitals. Linking 184 million claims from California's all-payer claims database (2018–2023) to federal provider registries, I identify each commercially insured patient's primary-care practice, specialists and surgeon, and reconstruct the sequence of care that leads to her hospital admission. I then re-estimate the willingness-to-pay model used in merger review, restricting each patient's choice set to the hospitals her practice routinely sends patients to. In gatekeeper plans, the referral-based model clears more than a quarter of the mergers the standard model flags. It also predicts that more than twice as many displaced patients stay within the same hospital system. In open-access plans, the two models predict nearly the same diversion.
