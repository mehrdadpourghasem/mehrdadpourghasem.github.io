Who Chooses Your Hospital? Inherited Demand and the Measurement of Hospital Competition

Hospital merger review rests on demand models in which patients choose hospitals. Using claims covering 8.5 million commercially insured Californians, I show that elective surgical demand is routed by referral menus belonging to primary care organizations: menus barely move when an organization's physicians are replaced, travel with physicians who change employer, and reallocate when organizations close. A consideration-set model places 2.4 of 48 available hospitals on the effective menu. Replaying observed closures, the standard model recovers 41% of measured within-system diversion against 88% for the menu model — a bias large enough to clear mergers that should be flagged.

Third-Year Paper

Heterogeneous Willingness-to-Pay for Electric Vehicles

Merged U.S. auto sales data with detailed vehicle attributes and estimated a random-coefficients logit (BLP) model with demographic interactions and micro-moments. Recovered willingness-to-pay for battery capacity and range, and quantified price sensitivity across household segments.

Road Infrastructure & Agricultural Outcomes

Built a county-level panel dataset linking village road access to agricultural land rents using newly compiled data. Produced spatial analyses and maps in ArcGIS to assess how improved road connectivity affected local land values and farming outcomes.
