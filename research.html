When Insurers Own Doctors: Referral Control and Hospital Prices

I estimate a structural model of hospital–insurer negotiation using Nash-in-Nash bargaining, together with models of household insurance choice and patient hospital demand, on 2018–2023 California claims and admissions data. Identification comes from the staggered timing of primary care contract transitions to capitation. The paper quantifies how insurer control of referral flows shifts bargaining leverage and negotiated hospital prices, and traces the resulting tradeoff between spending and quality.

Why Pay-for-Performance Failed to Move the Needle: Incentive Pass-Through in the U.S. Dialysis Industry

The ESRD Treatment Choices model — a mandatory, randomized Medicare payment reform launched in 2021 — produced essentially no change in home dialysis use. I build a facility-year panel of roughly 9,000 U.S. dialysis facilities from CMS claims and cost reports, and estimate a structural model that decomposes the null result into patient eligibility constraints, facility capacity and investment costs, and chain-level optimization across markets. I then use the model to evaluate counterfactual policies, including patient-support interventions, capacity subsidies, and payment-rate escalation.

Heterogeneous Willingness-to-Pay for Electric Vehicles

Merged U.S. auto sales data with detailed vehicle attributes and estimated a random-coefficients logit (BLP) model with demographic interactions and micro-moments. Recovered willingness-to-pay for battery capacity and range, and quantified price sensitivity across household segments.

Road Infrastructure & Agricultural Outcomes
